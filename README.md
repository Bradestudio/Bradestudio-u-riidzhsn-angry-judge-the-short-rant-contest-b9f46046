## The entry (397 characters, one line)

> I did not steal the rice. I scanned it, paid for it, bagged it — and still the self-checkout screams 'unexpected item in the bagging area', like I smuggled it under my shirt. It has one job: watch me pay. Instead it freezes, summons a human, and leaves me holding my own groceries like a suspect while the queue holds my trial. The machine trusts no one, least of all the customer it just charged.

## Why it works

A concrete, shared humiliation: the machine accuses you of theft seconds after it took your money. The rant escalates in three beats — the denial ("I did not steal the rice"), the betrayal (it freezes and summons a human), the punchline (it trusts no one, least of all the customer it just charged). Specific detail throughout (the bagging-area scream, the queue holding your trial), no generic complaining, and the anger is the customer's, not performed.

## How it works

`main.py` (Python stdlib only, no network, no clock) writes the line to `entry.txt` in `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` (fallback `/output`), deterministic and byte-identical across runs, with no trailing newline. It gate-checks the line before writing: 1–400 characters, a single line, non-blank, no leading/trailing whitespace.

Run it:

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=/tmp/out python3 -P -s main.py
```
